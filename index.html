Territorial Transformations

Context

Water, heritage, and transformation

This thesis asks how water-integrated architecture and territorial strategies can transform and renew heritage-sensitive landscapes. In many water-based territories, planning frameworks treat water primarily as technical infrastructure or environmental constraint, while development pressures promote replacement rather than transformation of existing buildings.

Using the Ohrid–Prespa region as a case study, the project repositions both water and existing building stock as active spatial resources. The region is framed as a mixed natural and cultural UNESCO landscape, shaped by biodiversity, long settlement continuity, tourism pressure, and overlapping governance structures.

Challenge

Can reuse replace expansion as the driver of growth in a water-heritage landscape?

The thesis challenges growth-dependent urbanisation, generic new-build infrastructure, and the demolition of existing building stock. It asks how adaptive reuse can transform vernacular and underused buildings into spatial and economic resources without degrading the environmental and cultural values that attract tourism in the first place.

Proposal

From expansion to sensitive transformation

The project operates across two interconnected scales: a strategic territorial prototype and an architectural prototype. Rather than introducing new development, it focuses on the reuse, transformation, and reactivation of existing, underused building stock, especially in areas peripheral to saturated tourist sites.

Strategic scale

A water-integrated strategic plan addressing long-term territorial challenges related to tourism, heritage protection, and environmental resilience.

Architectural scale

A case study translating regional insights into a concrete architectural and urban proposal based on reprogramming, adaptation, and reconnection rather than replacement.

Comparative lens

A small comparative analysis of similar interventions across Europe to position the project within a wider spatial and architectural discourse.

SDG alignment

The work aligns with UN Sustainable Development Goals 9, 11, and 17.

Site

A layered water-based territory

The thesis focuses on the Ohrid region within the wider Ohrid–Prespa transboundary landscape shared by North Macedonia, Albania, and Greece. The region is understood through geological formation, hydrological systems, civilisational layers, and current development pressures.

Trpejca

North Macedonia

A tourism-dependent rural settlement on the shoreline, relevant for testing pressure, seasonality, and water proximity.

Lin

Albania

A settlement with a more diversified economy, strong lakeside identity, and long archaeological continuity.

Shipokno

Galicica area

An abandoned village condition used to explore the reuse potential of underused rural stock in relation to the broader region.

Framework

Theoretical and methodological grounding

The project moves between territoriality, actor-networks, post-growth, doughnut urbanism, qualitative interviews, fieldwork, mapping, and architectural testing.

Post-growth planning and doughnut urban development
Epistemic tools: hand drawing, cartography, archives, interviews, cataloguing
Operative tools: GIS, parametric modelling, visualisation, CNC and 3D-printed models

Statement

Territorial transformations shift the focus from extractive expansion toward sensitive transformation, using water, heritage, and existing building stock as the basis for renewal in the Ohrid–Prespa landscape.
